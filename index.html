Smart India Hackathon is a nationwide innovation initiative that challenges students to solve pressing real‑world problems with practical, scalable technology. Team AEVORA 007 is competing in the Hardware category under the Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development theme.

!

The Problem

Small farmers lack a low‑cost way to monitor food crates during transport. Network failure and data tampering make product quality and traceability unreliable — spoilage is discovered only after the produce arrives, and by then the cause is untraceable.

✓

Our Solution

AGRI‑SEAL is a solar‑powered IoT monitoring node fixed to the transport vehicle, generating a single Batch/Trip QR code for all crates loaded together — cost‑effective, and it reflects the shared cargo environment every crate actually travels in.

Innovation

Proposed Solution

One node per vehicle. Signed at the source, chained on the card, delivered over whichever network is alive.

01

Integrated Hardware Stack

A solar‑charged battery, STM32F401 controller, ATECC608A security chip, SD card, 4G modem, LoRa module and SHT35 + MQ‑4 / MQ‑135 sensors (via ADS1115) monitor temperature, humidity and spoilage gases.

02

Cryptographic Integrity

Sensor data is cryptographically signed by the ATECC608A and hash‑chained on the SD card, producing tamper‑evident, trustworthy records that cannot be silently rewritten.

03

Never‑Offline Alerting

Alerts travel over 4G or LoRa, with automatic offline storage and sync during network outages — monitoring continues uninterrupted for the whole trip.

04

One Batch, One QR

The farmer app registers name, product, quantity, harvest date and place; the backend mints a single Batch/Trip ID and QR that any buyer can scan to read the full journey.

Uniqueness & Innovation

Dual‑Gas Monitoring

MQ‑135 + MQ‑4 via ADS1115 track ripening and spoilage accurately.

Tamper‑Proof Data

ATECC608A signing plus an SD hash‑chain detects tampering instantly.

Unified Batch Monitoring

One node and one QR per vehicle — low‑cost, and true to shared conditions.

Dual Connectivity

4G + LoRa fallback + offline SD backup means zero data loss.

Feasibility & Viability

Buildable Today, Affordable Tomorrow

Technical Feasibility

  • STM32F401, sensors, solar power, battery, 4G and LoRa modules integrate into one working system.
  • Provides secure real‑time monitoring and offline data storage during network failure.
  • Low‑cost, power‑efficient and suited to agricultural cargo — a whole vehicle load through a single node.

Operational Feasibility

  • Collects and records cargo conditions automatically, without continuous manual monitoring.
  • 4G, LoRa and offline SD storage keep it operating through network interruptions.
  • Real‑time alerts let operators respond quickly to temperature or spoilage changes.

Challenges Overcome

  • Affordable, with minimal maintenance requirements.
  • Solar‑powered design supports continuous operation without frequent manual charging.
  • One node per vehicle adapts easily to different crops, vehicles and transport conditions.
Impact & Benefits

Why It Matters

Social Impact

  • Reduces food spoilage during transportation.
  • Builds customer trust through transparent QR‑based traceability.
  • Improves food safety and supports rural digital development — post‑harvest losses cut deeply into small‑farmer income, which makes better cold‑chain monitoring socially important.

Benefits

  • Offline data storage prevents data loss during network failure.
  • Cryptographic hash‑chaining protects records from unauthorised modification.
  • Solar‑assisted power reduces battery replacement.
  • A single QR per vehicle gives quick, cost‑effective product‑history verification.
  • Reusable hardware reduces long‑term operating cost.

SWOT Analysis

Strength

Continuous offline monitoring ensures traceability data is preserved even during network outages.

Weakness

Sensor calibration may require periodic maintenance to keep readings reliable over long‑term deployment.

Opportunity

Can be scaled across multiple transport vehicles to create a wider farm‑to‑fork traceability network.

Threat

Availability of specific electronic components may vary over time, causing procurement delays.

Proof of Problem

Case Study — Field Insights

What farmers and transporters told us on the ground.

No temperature visibility

Farmers found produce rotten only after transit — the exact cause, a temperature deviation, was never known in real time.

No freshness estimate

Produce is stored purely on guesswork. Knowing the remaining shelf life would help store it for the right duration and cut waste.

No live tracking

Once dispatched, there is no way to know where the vehicle is or when it will arrive.

Takeaway: these are exactly the gaps AGRI‑SEAL closes — real‑time monitoring, freshness prediction and live GPS tracking.
